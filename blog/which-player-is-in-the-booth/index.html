Which player is in the booth?

Almost everything about preparing a USB is in your hands. One thing is not — and it decides the format you write.

One stick, two worlds

A DJ player does not read a folder of MP3s. It reads a library written in the format its own ecosystem uses: Engine OS on Denon DJ hardware, rekordbox on Pioneer DJ and AlphaTheta players. Both are perfectly ordinary USB sticks. They are not interchangeable.

Alpha asks you which one it should write right before it writes — the target format sits at the top of the Export step, and the app's own line there reads “Pick the controller format for your device - Alpha writes the matching library.” Picking it takes two seconds. Knowing what to pick is the part that happens days earlier, in a conversation with whoever booked you.

“CDJs” is not an answer

This is where most of the trouble starts. A promoter writes back “we have CDJs” and everyone considers the question settled. It isn't, because the differences that break a stick sit between models of the same family.

  • The CDJ-2000NXS2 reads FAT32 and HFS+ — and nothing else. Hand it an exFAT stick and it will keep asking you to format the drive.
  • The XDJ-1000MK2 and the XDJ-700 belong to the same generation: FAT32 or HFS+, no exFAT.
  • The XDJ-XZ, the OPUS-QUAD and the XDJ-AZ do read exFAT. The AZ has no SD slot at all, so USB is your only way in.
  • Newer AlphaTheta players lean on the encrypted Device Library Plus library rather than the classic one.

Four answers that all sounded like “CDJs”, four different sticks. Our compatibility list and the per-device guides spell out the quirks model by model — but they can only help once you know which line to read.

What to ask, in one message

You do not need a long exchange. You need five facts, and they fit in a single message:

  • The exact model of the players — including the suffix. NXS2 and MK2 are not decoration.
  • How many, and whether they are linked, because that changes whether one stick has to serve every deck.
  • Whether the booth runs Engine OS or rekordbox hardware, if the room has both.
  • Whether there is a window to test your stick on the actual player before doors.
  • Who is in the booth before you, since a changeover on one shared player is a different plan than two decks of your own.

Ask early. The answer costs the promoter thirty seconds a week before the show and nothing at all on the night; asked at 23:50 in the booth, it costs everybody.

Where that answer actually lives

From the organiser's side, this is not an obscure detail — it is a line item. The booth spec belongs to the technical rider, and the rider belongs to the booking. Promoters who run their events on Alpha Events have it in one place: their artist booking module matches the stage equipment against the technical riders, so the gear list and your requirements are compared before anyone drives to the venue.

For you as the booked artist, the useful half is the artist portal: you edit your own rider there instead of attaching a PDF to a mail thread, and each artist gets a day sheet with arrival, soundcheck and set times. If your rider says “Engine OS preferred, otherwise please confirm rekordbox model”, that sentence is sitting in the promoter's workflow rather than in an inbox.

It is worth putting the booth question into your rider once and never typing it again. Alpha Events is a German platform with a free tier, which makes it an easy thing to point a promoter at when they are still running the night out of a spreadsheet.

When nobody answers

Sometimes the reply never comes. Then you prepare for the worst plausible booth:

  • Format the stick FAT32. It is the one file system every player in both families reads. exFAT buys you nothing on a night where you do not know the model.
  • Carry two identical sticks. Not a backup of your laptop — a second stick, written the same way, in your other pocket.
  • Write both formats if the brand is genuinely unknown. In Alpha the target is a chooser at the Export step, so writing the same set again for the other ecosystem is one more pass, not a second evening of work.
  • Get to the booth early enough to load one track. Thirty seconds on the real player answers what no message did.

The short version

Everything on a prepared stick is your decision — the tracks, the order, the levels. The format is the exception: it is a property of a booth you have never seen. Ask for it in writing, keep it in your rider, and write FAT32 when the room stays silent.
